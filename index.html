Self-hosted internet radio

GenWave

Turns your own music library into a real radio station: one shared broadcast stream, loudness-matched so quiet and loud tracks play back even, crossfaded so the silence never happens.

And there's a DJ — one who actually knows what's playing, and tells you about it between the tracks.

On air in 1:03

Measured 2026-08-19: Raspberry Pi 5 (4 GB), 9,000-track library on NFS — 1:36 to the first note. Fresh VM: 1:10.

One line. On air.

Docker and a music library are the only prerequisites. This clones GenWave at its latest release and hands off to its own setup wizard — no sudo, anywhere in the path.

curl -fsSL https://install.genwaveradio.com/install.sh | bash

Shortens to just curl -fsSL https://install.genwaveradio.com | bash once the redirect rule is in place — it's the identical script either way, and the full path above works today.

Standing on

      
GenWave is a control plane, not a from-scratch audio engine. It's wired around real infrastructure that did the hard parts first.

      
    
        
  • 
          Liquidsoap
          
    Real-time mixing, crossfade, and encode — the actual audio spine underneath everything GenWave decides to play.
    
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Icecast
          
    Fans the mixed stream out to every listener, reliably, at whatever scale the station needs.
    
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Kokoro
          
    The primary DJ voice — an open-weight TTS model, rendered locally, no cloud voice API.
    
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Piper
          
    A fast, CPU-only local voice — the fallback that keeps a DJ talking on modest hardware.
    
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Ollama
          
    Runs the local LLM that writes lead-ins and back-announces when you point GenWave at one — the station airs template patter without it.
    
        
    • 
        
  • 
          ffmpeg
          
    Loudness, cue points, energy analysis — the numbers every crossfade decision rests on.
    
        
    • 
        
  • 
          aubio
          
    The BPM analysis under the hood, one more measurement the catalog remembers per track.
    
        
    • 
        
  • 
          PostgreSQL
          
    The catalog of record — every track, every measurement, every scheduled show.
